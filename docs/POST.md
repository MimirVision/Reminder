# Post: a mail app for your iPhone (a Home Screen web app, free)

Post is a fast, calm replacement for Apple Mail for your **Outlook** accounts (personal and work). It lives at `/post/` on the same web hosting as Home Memory.
On a phone you add it to your Home Screen and it shows **the number on its icon** when new mail has arrived; on a computer it is a three-pane mail app with keyboard shortcuts.
You sign in with one button, **Continue with Microsoft**, the same on a phone and a computer. No Mac, no Sideloadly, no 7-day re-install, nothing paid.

The look is the "A2" design on the canvas: https://claude.ai/artifact/QCFREjFYyX2YG2T91zTQGq

```
New mail arrives in Outlook
   -> Microsoft calls your Supabase function (post-alerts)            within seconds
   -> it decides with the app's own sorting (Primary mail: yes. a promotion or an update: no. work account after hours: no)
   -> a Web Push through Apple to the Post icon on your Home Screen
   -> iOS shows the number on the icon (nothing else, if you choose Badges only)
   -> open Post: the number clears, your mail is already there
```

## What it does
- **One inbox for personal and work**, with a small coloured letter on each row. Mail is sorted into four tabs like iOS Mail: **Primary** (people), **Transactions** (receipts, invoices, bookings), **Updates** (alerts and notifications) and **Promotions** (offers and newsletters, with a **Clean up** button), plus **All**. Sorting reads the sender, the subject, and the hidden bulk-mail marks in each message's headers, and it learns the people you have written to. Nothing is ever hidden: **All** shows everything, and "Why is this here?" explains each sorting and moves a sender (or a whole company) to another tab in one tap. Settings, Sorting lists your rules (the icon number follows these tabs too, see below) and can copy a privacy-safe report.
- **Who it is from**: the reader shows the sender's name and their **full address**, and one tap on the address copies it. Your own address is left out unless you have several accounts connected (then it shows which account a message is in).
- **Conversations**: messages that answer each other are **one row** in the list (a small number says how many) and **one conversation** when you open it. The newest message is open and the older ones are folded up under it (tap one to read it). **Your own answers are in it too**, marked "You", because Post fetches them from your Sent Items. Under each open message are **Reply**, **Reply all** and **Forward** for that very message (the Reply at the bottom answers the newest message that is not yours). Archive, delete, flag and read/unread take the **whole conversation** with one **Undo**,. **Clean up** in Promotions works on the rows you see there, so a conversation goes as a whole, and never one that has a flagged message in it. The rest of a conversation (your answers, messages archived or in other folders, mail older than what is on the phone) comes from Outlook when you open it, never from Drafts, Deleted Items or Junk, and is **not saved on the phone**. Settings, **Conversations**, **Group replies together** turns all of this off, and every message gets its own row again.
- **Files, both ways**: every file on a message is listed. **PDFs, pictures and text files open inside Post**; Word, Excel, zip and the rest are saved or opened in another app with one tap (on an iPhone that is the share sheet, with Save to Files). To send files, tap the paperclip in a new message, a reply or a forward (or drag files in, or paste a picture on a computer). Up to **20 MB** per message; files over 3 MB go to Outlook in slices, and **Undo** gives the message and its files back.
- **Fast triage**: swipe right to archive, left to flag (both configurable in Settings, Swipe: Archive, Mark read, Flag or Delete; Flag, Delete or Mark read for left), **Undo** on every action, "Archive · next" in the reader, select several at once, and **Triage mode** (one message at a time: Archive, Mark read, Reply later, Reply now; drag the card right to archive, left to mark it read). On a phone, **Triage** is a button at the top of the inbox, next to Select (on a computer it is in the sidebar). It goes through the **unread** conversations of the tab you are in and the mailbox you chose, newest first, so from **Promotions** it clears promotions. A message you flagged with Reply later is already waiting in **Later** and is not dealt out again. The button is there only when something is unread in that tab, it is hidden while you are selecting messages, and for VoiceOver it says how many there are.
- **A flick to All**: the row of tabs scrolls under your finger as usual. Only a hard, quick flick to the left across it jumps straight to **All**, like swiping all the way in iOS Mail; a normal drag, a slow one or one to the right only scrolls the row. Tapping a tab still works, and the list slides in from the side you came from. There is **no Snooze** any more (Settings, Swipe, has Flag, Delete and Mark read for left); mail that an older version snoozed is simply back in the inbox.
- **Smoother, and a calmer icon**: sheets, toasts, pages, the action bars and the Triage cards move with one easy-out curve, and nothing moves when the phone is set to **Reduce Motion**. The Home Screen icon is a dark P with an orange check on cream, with no round dot that could look like new mail (remove the old icon from the Home Screen and add Post again to see it; the picture is cached by iOS).
- **One row per sender**: in **Transactions, Updates and Promotions** all of one sender's mail is one row ("Butikken", a small number for how many messages, and "Also: ..." naming what else is in it). Tap it to open it up and see the conversations (tap again to fold it); tap one of those to read it. Swipe the row, or tick it in Select, and the action reaches all of that sender's mail with one Undo and one toast ("Archived 6"). A conversation you flagged stays a row of its own, and **Primary** and **All** are never grouped. Settings, **Conversations**, **One row per sender** turns it off. Clean up, Triage and the counts still work on single conversations.
- **Sweep this sender**: in the reader, **More, Sweep this sender** clears what that sender has piled up in your inbox, in one move with one Undo: **Archive everything**, **Keep the newest, archive the rest**, **Archive what is older than 10 days**, or **Delete everything** (to Deleted, not for good). Each choice shows how many conversations it reaches. For a company (not a shared provider such as gmail.com) you can switch to **All of the company**. Mail you flagged is never swept, and it only looks at the inbox on the phone (what Post shows in All).
- **Subscriptions**: Settings, **Subscriptions** lists who mails you the most: every sender of newsletters and offers (mail with an unsubscribe link, a mailing-list mark, or sorted as a promotion) in the inbox on the phone, busiest first, with how many mails, about how many a week and how many are unread. **Never opened** shows senders with two or more mails where you have not opened one. Tap a sender for **See their mail**, **Sweep**, **Unsubscribe** (opens their page, or sends the request from your account; shown only when their mail has a link) and **Block**. Mail from people is never listed. Counts cover the inbox on the phone, not all of Outlook.
- **Mute conversation**: in the reader, **More, Mute conversation** archives that conversation at once (one toast, one Undo) and archives every later reply to it, whenever Post is open, so it stops filling the inbox. Mail you flagged or brought back to the inbox stays. Settings, **Sorting**, **Muted conversations** lists them with **Unmute**. Nothing is deleted. It is done by Post itself: a reply that arrives while Post is closed can still give one alert and a number on the icon, and is archived the next time Post is open.
- **Where did my mail go?** (Settings, This device): a written record of the last 80 messages that left the inbox list or moved to another tab, with who did it: you in Post (archive, delete, move), a blocked sender, a muted conversation, the old-promotion tidy, a tab choice of yours, or **Moved or deleted at Outlook, not by Post** (a rule, the junk filter, another app). Tap an entry that is still in the inbox to open it. It is also counted (no subjects or names) in Health's problem report. New mail is now sorted by its hidden marks for up to 2.5 seconds before it is first shown, so a newsletter no longer shows in Primary for a moment and then moves to Promotions. When Outlook itself moved a message, Post asks Outlook where it went (sender and arrival time, across every folder) and the entry says which folder, with a tap to open it. A message that moves tab while you are looking at its tab says so in one line ("Ukens nyheter moved to Updates") with an Undo that puts the sender back in Primary.
- **Trash can on each row**: a small can at the side of every row in the inbox and in folders (Archive, Junk, Drafts ...; not in Deleted, where a second delete would be for good) deletes the message without opening it, with the usual Undo. Always there on a phone; on a computer it appears when the pointer is over the row. Hidden while you are selecting, and on a sender's summary row (open it to delete one conversation).
- **New folders and subfolders, and dragging mail into folders**: **New folder** sits at the top of each mailbox on the Folders tab, as a **+** beside Folders in the computer sidebar, as **New folder…** at the end of **Move to…**, and as **New subfolder here** (the **+** at the top) inside a folder of yours. You give a name and choose **Put it** at the top, inside Inbox or inside one of your folders (two levels deep at most, as far as the Folders tab reads). It is made in Outlook itself, so it shows up there and on your other devices. A name that exists already beside it is refused in plain words and nothing is made twice. From Move to… or the strip below, **Make folder and move** makes the folder and moves the messages into it in one go. **On a phone, hold a row for about half a second**: it lifts, a strip of places slides in (Inbox when it is not there, Archive, Junk, New folder…, then your folders) and you let go on one of them; let go anywhere else and the strip stays so you can tap one, or tap **Cancel**. Scrolling and swiping a row still work as before. A sender's summary row cannot be lifted (open it first). **On a computer**, drag a row with the mouse onto Inbox, Archive, Junk or one of your folders in the sidebar (they light up where a drop is allowed; Drafts and Sent never take one). Every drop has the same toast and **Undo** as Move to….
- **Search results work like the inbox**: in a search, every result (on the phone or older from Outlook) has the little trash can, the swipes of the place it is in, **Select** (the button next to the search field: Select all, the big button, Mark read, Move to…, Delete), and hold-and-drag to a folder on a phone, or a drag onto the sidebar on a computer. Drafts and mail already in Deleted only offer what makes sense there.
- **Drag several at once, and the search stays**: while you are picking (Select), hold one of the picked rows (or drag it with the mouse): everything picked goes with it, and the strip says how many (a row you hold that is not picked comes along too). Open a search result and go back, and the search is still there with its words and, if you asked Outlook, its older results; on a computer the search stays in the list while the result is open. Going to the inbox, a folder or another screen ends the search, so a new visit to Search starts empty. Selecting in a search puts the keyboard away.
- **Folders and Drafts**: a fourth tab, **Folders**, lists **Inbox, Drafts, Sent, Archive, Junk, Deleted** and the folders you made in Outlook (a folder inside another is indented). Open one to read what Outlook has there, 40 messages at a time with **Show more** for the rest (sent mail says who it went **To**). What you do in the inbox works there too, with the same **Undo**: swipe, the big button (in Archive it puts a message back in the inbox, in Junk it says **Not junk**, in Deleted **Restore**), **Move to…** (Archive, Junk, Deleted, then your own folders, each one tap), read/unread and flag, **Next/Previous** through the folder, and a reply to something you sent goes to the person you wrote to. **A draft opens in the editor**: change the text, the people or the subject, then **Save** it to Outlook or **Send** it (it goes once, with Undo, and the files already on it go with it). Only what you changed is written to the draft, and files you add are put on it when you save or send. A draft with someone only in Cc or Bcc can be sent. If the draft was changed in Outlook after the changes you had not saved here (and the two really differ), Post asks which one to go on with (**Keep my changes** means everything you see on screen replaces the draft in Outlook), and if it is gone from Outlook (sent or deleted in another app) Post says so and keeps what you wrote as a message of its own. A message you were writing on this phone and had not saved is listed under Drafts too, and it never mixes with the changes to a draft. Search says which folder each result is in.
- **Tidying folders**: in any folder tap **Select** (the ticked-list button next to the refresh button) to pick several messages, then use the same bar as in the inbox: **Select all** (the messages listed so far), the big button (Inbox, Not junk, Restore or Archive, by folder), **Mark read**, **Move to…** and **Delete**, with the usual **Undo**. In Drafts only Delete is offered. **Junk** and **Deleted** also have an **Empty** bar at the top: it says how many messages Outlook holds there (not only the 40 on screen), asks once, and then deletes them **for good**: they do not go to Deleted, there is no Undo, and Post cannot bring them back. While it goes a notice at the top says how far it is (keep Post open). In **Deleted** the bar's last button is **Delete for good** (same question, for the messages you picked). With two mailboxes and "All accounts" the question says it empties all of them; choose one mailbox first to empty only that one. If Outlook refuses, Post stops after the first group, says how many are gone, and the rest stays listed.
- **Block a sender, and clean up promotions by itself**: in the reader, **More, Block sender** offers *this address* or *everything from the company* (not for a shared provider such as gmail.com: there only the one address). Their mail in the inbox goes to **Junk** at once (with **Undo**, which also lifts the block), and so does whatever they send later **while Post is open**. Nothing is deleted, mail you flagged is left alone, and mail you bring back from Junk with Not junk stays where you put it. Blocked senders are also sorted as Promotions, so they never add to the icon number. **Settings, Sorting, Blocked senders** lists them with **Unblock**. Below it, **Clean up old promotions, Archive by itself** (Off, or after 3, 7, 14 or 30 days; **Off** to begin with) archives old promotions while Post is open, with an Undo; it never touches flagged mail, mail whose tab is still only a first guess, or mail you brought back. The **Clean up** sheet in Promotions links to the same choice.
- **The number on the icon follows your Primary tab.** The server sorts each new mail the way the app does, so a promotion, a newsletter or an update never adds to the number, and neither does a sender or company you moved to another tab (**Why is this here?**; every phone and computer you use adds its moves to one list that the server keeps for the mailbox, and Undo and removed rules reach it too). People on your VIP list still get through, even a sender you moved. **One-time codes and sign-in alerts count too**, because you want to hear those; Settings, **Icon number & alerts**, your mailbox, **Besides Primary, also count** changes that to **Nothing** (Primary only) or **Transactions** (receipts, deliveries and bookings too). Each mailbox also has **alert hours** (for example work only Mon to Fri 07:30 to 17:00) and a **VIP list** that always gets through.
- **Mail to reminder**: "Remind me" in the reader finds the date in the message and hands it to Home Memory.
- **Search** that runs on the phone (instant, works offline, understands æ ø å) with `from:`, `is:unread`, `has:attachment`, `in:promotions` (or primary, transactions, updates), `account:work`, plus a button to search all of Outlook (those results open too, so you can read, reply to or forward an old message).
- **Privacy**: remote images (tracking pixels) blocked until you tap Load once, no read receipts, no "Sent from Post", and your mail is shown in a sandbox that cannot run scripts.
- **Reliable**: opens instantly from what is on the phone (a slow or broken network opens the saved copy after three seconds), works offline, and every action waits safely in a queue until Outlook confirms it. **A message goes out exactly once**: it is made as a draft at Outlook first, so if the answer to "send" is lost on the way back Post asks Outlook what happened instead of sending again. Every call to Outlook and to your server has a time limit, so one stuck connection can never freeze Post. Drafts are saved as you type. When you leave Post (another app, the lock screen, closing it) what is waiting for its undo time goes at once, because an iPhone may not run Post again for hours. If the phone refuses to keep Post's copy of your mail, Post says so and keeps working. A screen that breaks shows a way out, not a white page. **Settings, Health** says in plain words how Post is doing, fixes the usual things, and copies a problem report with no mail in it.
- **Seasonal touches** (Settings, **Appearance**, **Seasonal touches**): a few small things that fall slowly past the screen, a faint wash of colour at the top, and a tiny badge on the corner of people's pictures (not on automatic senders). Automatic follows Norway's calendar: winter, New Year, Valentine's Day, Easter, spring, 17 May, summer, autumn, Halloween (25 Oct to 1 Nov), Christmas (from 1 Dec); or pick one season, or Off. Falling pieces, colours and badge each have their own switch, and the pieces never show when the phone is set to reduce motion.
- **Two looks**, the same mail and the same buttons (Settings, **Appearance**, **Look**): **Refined**, warm and rounded, where every sender keeps one colour (a round letter for a person, a rounded square for shops, banks and automatic mail), and **Calm**, plain and quiet like the apps that come with the iPhone (the phone's own font, thin lines between messages, blue). Both come in light, dark and pure black, with 8 accent colours (Calm starts with Ocean; a colour you picked yourself stays when you change look), three row sizes and a colour per account. Mail itself stays on a white page in dark mode, because it is written for one.
- **Phone and computer**: on a window wider than about 900 px Post shows a sidebar, the message list and the reading pane side by side, with keyboard shortcuts (`j`/`k`, `e` archive, `r` reply, `c` write, `/` search, `?` for the list). When the window is about 1220 px or wider, the actions for the open message (Archive, Reply, Reply all, Forward, Delete, More, with the key in the hover text) are a bar along the top of the reading pane; a narrower window keeps the floating bar of the phone. On the phone the three icons under a message have their names written under them.

## The honest limits
- **iPhone, iOS 16.4 or newer**, and the icon number only works from the **Home Screen icon**.
- **The number counts alerts since you last opened Post.** iOS only lets a web app change its number when a notification is delivered, so the number **cannot go down by itself** if you read mail elsewhere (Outlook on your PC). It corrects as soon as you open Post, and reading mail inside Post clears it.
- iOS requires every web push to be a notification. If you choose **Badges only** in iOS Settings (below) you will see just the number, no banners, no sounds.
- **Following the Primary tab needs the newest server code**, pasted once into Supabase (step 3, "Updating the alert server"; no SQL). Until then the server keeps its older rule (people yes, newsletters and automatic mail no), Settings, **Icon number & alerts** shows **Needs update** next to **Alerts follow your Primary tab** and calls the mode **People** instead of **Primary**, and Post sends the server nothing new. Nothing breaks: the number just does not yet follow the tabs.
- **The server sorts from less than the phone knows.** When a mail arrives the server sees the sender, the subject, the hidden bulk-mail marks and the first lines of the message; the phone has the whole message and everyone you have written to. So the server and the app can sort a mail differently now and then (the icon number is the server's call, the tab is the app's). When the server cannot tell, the mail counts: a missed alert is worse than an extra one. To catch people who write to you after you wrote to them, the server asks Outlook whether you have ever written to the sender (Sent Items, **To** only: a mail where you were only on Cc does not make someone a person you write to), but only when the answer would change the verdict, for example a contractor sending an invoice, never for a no-reply style address, and once for a burst of mail from the same sender. Outlook gets five seconds to answer; if it does not, the mail counts. That lookup is the one part that has not been tried against a real mailbox yet.
- **A business that writes like a person is sorted by its words, not its name.** A clinic's own address sending an appointment reminder is **Transactions**, so with the default (**Codes**) it does not count. If you want it, move that sender to Primary (**Why is this here?**), or choose **Transactions** under **Besides Primary, also count**. A one-time code from a sender you moved to Transactions still counts; one from a sender you moved to Promotions does not.
- **A code is recognised by its words, not by "code" and a number.** "Engangskode", "verification code", "security code", or digits after "Your ... code is" or "... is your ... code" or "confirmation code 123-456" ("Your Apple ID Code is: 482913", "482913 is your Instagram code", "Din kode er 482913") are codes; a postal, tracking, booking, ticket, invite, referral or promo code is not. A sign-in alert or a password mail ("Security alert", "new device", "reset your password") counts when it comes from a no-reply style sender and sells nothing. Mail sent in bulk (an unsubscribe link, a mailing list) counts only when it carries a code: a newsletter that talks about signing in goes to Updates, and so does a sign-in alert that comes with an unsubscribe link (that is the price of keeping newsletters out). A code mail the words do not catch (letters only, say) is sorted as an update and does not count; move that sender to Primary once (**Why is this here?**) and its mail does.
- **Your moves reach the server through the phone.** Post sends them as you make them, and only what changed, so a computer that has moved nothing never undoes what the phone moved. If a device was offline at that moment (or the server lost them), it sends them again by itself the next time it is open, and until then the server judges new mail by the older choices. The server holds up to 1 000 moves per mailbox, more than anyone makes.
- Each device signs in on its own (Safari and the Home Screen icon do not share a sign-in on iPhone), so the first time you open the Home Screen icon you press the same button again. It takes ten seconds.
- Google/Gmail and iCloud are not supported yet: **Continue with Microsoft** is the only button, because Outlook is what Post is built for.
- Your own Supabase keeps an **encrypted sign-in** for each account (so it can see new mail arriving, and so the app does not have to ask you to sign in every day). It is yours, and removing the account in Post deletes it. A device proves it belongs to a mailbox with a secret it gets at sign-in (only a hash is stored); only the addresses you allow (`post_setup` / `post_allow`) can sign in at all.
- **Undo lasts while Post is on screen.** The moment you leave Post, what waits for its undo time is sent, so a message you write and then walk away from is never stuck on a phone that has put Post to sleep. On a computer, switching to another tab does not send early; closing the tab does. (Settings says this under Undo send.)
- **A draft can be left behind in rare cases.** A message is made as a draft at Outlook before it is sent. If the connection drops at the very moment the draft is made, Outlook may keep a draft that Post never sent; it is harmless and can be deleted in Outlook's Drafts.
- **Files**: an iPhone gives a Home Screen app no way to keep a half-sent upload alive in the background, so for a big file keep Post open until it says Sent (a message with files that cannot be sent is handed back as a draft with its files and the reason). Word, Excel and PowerPoint files are not drawn by Post; they are saved or opened in the apps that can.
- **Conversations** follow the conversation Outlook itself reports, so if someone changes the subject and Outlook starts a new conversation, it is a new row. Post fetches at most 100 messages of one conversation from Outlook (it does not choose which, so a thread longer than that may miss some older ones). Your own answers and older messages are fetched when you open it, so without a connection you see the messages that are on the phone and a note with **Try again**. Replying from a conversation answers the newest message that is not yours.
- **Folders** are read from Outlook when you open one, so they need a connection (the list of folders is remembered, the mail in them is not saved on the phone). Not in this version: making, renaming or deleting folders, emptying a folder of your own or Archive, deleting one message for good from the reader, searching inside one folder (Search looks in all of them), and Outlook's own extras (Outbox, Sync Issues, Conversation History, Clutter and the like are left out of the list; if Outlook has other special folders of its own, one may still show up).
- **Drafts** are edited as plain text, so a draft written in Outlook with fonts or pictures in the text loses them if you change its text and save or send it from Post (the editor says so; if you change only the subject or the people, the text stays as Outlook has it). A file that is already on a draft stays and goes with it, but Post cannot take it off (do that in Outlook). The Bcc of a draft is shown and kept, never changed. Changes you have not saved stay on this phone (for 30 days, then they are let go of); **Cancel** asks whether to save them to the draft or throw them away. A big file added to a draft takes a while to save: keep Post open until the question closes. If a draft is deleted or sent in another app at the very moment you send it from here, what you wrote comes back as the message you are writing, and if you had started another message meanwhile that one is replaced (the same is true when Outlook refuses a new message). If a draft is sent or deleted in another app after you changed it here without saving, those changes are not listed anywhere and are let go of after 30 days.
- **Blocking happens in Post, not in Outlook.** Post asks Outlook for no new permission, so there is no Outlook rule: a blocked sender's mail is moved to Junk when Post is open (right after it reads and sorts your mail). While Post is closed, new mail from them waits in Outlook's inbox until the next time you open Post, and other mail apps still show it there. (An Outlook rule would need one more permission, *MailboxSettings.ReadWrite*, and everyone would have to consent again: not in this version.) Automatic clean-up is the same: it only runs while Post is open.
- Not in this version: rules, Gmail/iCloud (Outlook only for now).
- **Work accounts** (Microsoft 365): your employer may need to approve the app. If the sign-in says "approval required", that is why.

## One-time setup (about 15 minutes, once, for everyone who will use it)
Four steps. There are no secrets to type into Supabase and no schedule to create: the database does both, and the app finds its server by itself.

### 1. Microsoft: get a client ID
Microsoft will only let an app sign people in once the app is registered, and registering needs a Microsoft Entra directory (a "tenant").
- **You have a work or school Microsoft 365 account that lets you register apps:** use it, or skip to "The portal way" below.
- **You only have a personal account (Outlook.com, Hotmail, Live):** a personal account has no directory, so the registration form is closed to it. The free fix is a free Azure account, which creates a directory for you: https://azure.microsoft.com/free, sign in with your personal Microsoft account. It asks for a phone number and a card as an identity check (a temporary hold, no charge). Registering an app costs nothing, now or after the free period ends. This is a Microsoft rule, not something Post can work around.

Then register the app with one command:
1. Open **Azure Cloud Shell**: https://shell.azure.com, choose **Bash**. It is already signed in as you.
2. Paste `scripts/post-azure.sh` (or its text) and run it with the address of Post: `bash post-azure.sh https://<your web address>/post/` (the slash at the end matters).
3. It prints your **Application (client) ID**. That is all Microsoft needs.

<details><summary>The portal way, if you prefer clicking</summary>

1. https://entra.microsoft.com, **App registrations, New registration**: name `Post`, supported account types **Accounts in any organizational directory and personal Microsoft accounts**, redirect URI platform **Mobile and desktop applications** (not "Single-page application": those sign-ins expire after 24 hours, these last 90 days) with value `https://<your web address>/post/`.
2. **API permissions, Add a permission, Microsoft Graph, Delegated**: `User.Read`, `Mail.Read`, `Mail.ReadWrite`, `Mail.Send`, `offline_access`.
3. **Authentication**: **Allow public client flows: Yes**. Save. Copy the **Application (client) ID**.

### 2. Supabase: the database
In the Supabase SQL Editor run `supabase/post.sql` (only Post's own tables and helpers; safe to repeat), then:

```sql
select post_setup('<your Application (client) ID>', 'you@outlook.com');
```
That stores the client ID and the address that may sign in (use the address Microsoft reports; if one is refused the message says which). To let another mailbox in later: `select post_allow('you@yourfirm.no');`. Only addresses on that list can sign in at all, so a stranger who finds your server cannot register a mailbox or receive alerts.

### 3. Supabase: the server
Edge Functions, **Deploy a new function, Via Editor**: name `post-alerts`, paste all of `supabase/dashboard/post-alerts.ts`, turn **Verify JWT OFF**, Deploy. (Updating? Paste it again over the old one.)

The function makes what it needs by itself the first time it runs: the key that seals sign-ins is derived from the service role key it already has, the Web Push key pair is generated and kept in the database (or taken from `VAPID_PUBLIC_KEY` / `VAPID_PRIVATE_KEY` if you set them for Home Memory), and the database asks it every 6 hours to renew the alert subscriptions (Microsoft ends them after under 3 days for personal accounts). Opening Post renews them too. If your project has no `pg_cron`, only the opening-Post renewal applies.

Supabase secrets still work and win when present (`MS_CLIENT_ID`, `POST_ALLOWED_EMAILS`, `ALERTS_ENC_KEY`, `POST_ALERTS_KEY`, `VAPID_*`), for anyone who prefers them.

**Updating the alert server.** When a new version of Post changes the server, Settings, **Icon number & alerts** shows **Needs update** next to **Alerts follow your Primary tab**. Updating is one paste and no SQL:
1. In Supabase open **Edge Functions**, **post-alerts**, then **Code**.
2. Select everything in the editor, delete it, and paste the whole of `supabase/dashboard/post-alerts.ts` (on GitHub open the file, press **Raw**, select all, copy).

## Troubleshooting
- **"The redirect address is not set up in Azure"**: the redirect URI must be exactly `https://<your web address>/post/` under *Mobile and desktop applications*.
- **"Your organisation needs to approve Post"**: ask your IT admin to grant consent for the app, or use the account without it.
- **"… is not on this server's allowed list"**: run `select post_allow('that address');` in the Supabase SQL editor (then try again).
- **"Post's server has no Microsoft client ID yet"**: run `select post_setup('<client id>', 'you@outlook.com');` in the Supabase SQL editor (step 2).
- **"Supabase is blocking Post's server"**: **Verify JWT** is still on for the function. In Supabase open Edge Functions, `post-alerts`, turn **Verify JWT** off, and try again.
- **A big file does not send**: Post first tries to hand the file to Outlook straight from the browser, and if the browser is not allowed to, through this site's own address `/api/post-upload` (it only passes on slices of files to Outlook's upload address, nothing else). If both fail, the message comes back as a draft with its files and the reason shown. After updating, make sure the latest version of the site has been published (it is part of the same Cloudflare deploy as Post).
- **"The list of files could not be loaded"** under a sender: Outlook did not answer; tap **Try again**.
- **"Could not look for the rest of this conversation"**: Outlook did not answer (or you are offline). The messages on the phone are shown; tap **Try again**.
- **A folder says it could not be read**: Outlook did not answer (or you are offline). Tap **Try again** (or **Show more** if it is the next page that failed). The inbox works without a connection; the folders do not, because their mail is read from Outlook when you open them.
- **"This draft is not in Outlook any more"** (or "…is no longer in Outlook" when you save or send): it was sent or deleted in another app. If you were sending it, what you wrote is kept under Drafts as a message that is **Not in Outlook yet** and can be sent as a new message; if you were saving it, the changes stay on the screen so you can copy them.
- **A conversation is missing your own answer**: only answers Outlook has in Sent Items (or another folder except Drafts, Deleted Items and Junk) are fetched, and Post fetches at most 100 messages of a conversation. If someone changed the subject, Outlook may have started a new conversation.
- **Settings, Alerts says "Mail only"**: Microsoft would not start new-mail alerts for that mailbox; the reason is shown under it, and your mail works as normal. Post tries again every 6 hours and whenever you open it.
- **Banner "Sign in again"**: Microsoft revoked the sign-in (password change, policy). Tap Sign in; nothing is lost.
- **No number appears**: Settings, Icon number & alerts shows its checks (on Home Screen, notifications allowed, server answers, alerts follow the Primary tab). Supabase, Edge Functions, `post-alerts`, Logs show one line for each new mail, with the reason and the sender's domain (never a subject, a name or an address), for example `alerted (a person) · from gmail.com`, `alerted (a security code or sign-in alert) · from github.com`, `skipped: not in Primary (a promotion) · from shop.no`, `skipped: outside this account’s alert hours · from firma.no`, `skipped: already read · from gmail.com`.
- **Settings, Icon number & alerts says "Needs update"** on the check **Alerts follow your Primary tab**: the server still has the older code. Paste the newest `supabase/dashboard/post-alerts.ts` (step 3, "Updating the alert server"), then open Post once.
- **I got a number for a mail that is not in Primary, or none for one that is**: the server decided from what it saw when the mail arrived (see the honest limits). Move the sender with **Why is this here?** and the server hears about it within seconds; the Logs line for that mail says why it counted or not. A mail you want that does not count is usually a business writing like a person (a clinic, a school): move it to Primary, or choose **Transactions** under **Besides Primary, also count**.
- **The number stays after you read mail elsewhere**: see the honest limits. Open Post to clear it.
- **Something is not right**: open Settings, **Health**. The top says what needs a look (a mailbox that needs a sign-in, a message that has been waiting to be sent, a read of your mail that failed, the phone running out of room). **Read my mail now** reads again, **Read all mail again from Outlook** starts the inbox over (what waits to be sent is kept) and is the cure when the phone shows something different from Outlook, **Start Post over** loads Post's own files again (mail and settings are not touched), and **Copy problem report** copies versions, times and counts, never subjects, names, addresses or message text.
- **"Post cannot save on this phone right now"**: the phone or browser would not give Post storage (private browsing, blocked site data, a full phone). Everything still works and your mail is safe in Outlook, but what you do is kept only until Post is closed. Closing Post completely and opening it again usually fixes it; a private browser window never keeps anything.
- **"Something went wrong on this screen"**: **Back to the inbox** tries again, **Read my mail again** rebuilds the list from Outlook, **Start Post over** loads Post afresh. If it keeps happening, **Copy problem report** and send it.
- **A message is not going out**: the inbox says "waiting to be sent" and Health says for how long. Post tries every minute while it is open, and again whenever you come back to it.
- The status of each watched mailbox (and "last alert 3 min ago") is under Settings, Icon number & alerts, Watching.

## For developers
- `apps/web/src/post/core/` has no browser or React code and is tested in Node: `cd apps/web && npm test` (Graph client with retries and token refresh, delta sync, undo queue, outbox, classification, search, conversations, sign-in, the controller against a fake Outlook).
- Conversations: `core/threads.ts` groups messages by account and Outlook's `conversationId` (pure, tested in `threads.test.ts`); the inbox, Later and the counts are built from those groups in `core/controller.ts` (`visibleThreads`, `mailCounts`). The rest of a conversation is `graph.conversation()` (`GET /me/messages?$filter=conversationId eq '…'`, never `$orderby`, which Microsoft refuses) and `controller.loadConversation()`; those messages are kept in memory only and are marked `folder: 'archive'` ("not in the inbox on this phone").
- Reliability: every call has a time limit (`core/graph.ts` `within()`: Graph 30 s, files and long jobs four times that; `core/server.ts` 20 s; the answer is read inside the limit), and a call that changes something (`send`) is never repeated blindly (`once`). All sending goes through `graph.deliver()`: make a draft, add files one by one (checking what is already on the draft after a lost answer), ask Outlook to send once; its progress (`made`, `sending`) is saved with the message in the outbox so a try that was cut short is carried on, never repeated, and a draft that is gone after "sending" means it went. `core/queue.ts` runs one flush at a time and reports a moved message's new name (`onMoved`, so a reply waiting for a message that was archived follows it) and an action Outlook refuses for good (`onRefused`, which reads that mailbox again and tells you). `controller.leaving()` first waits for what was tapped a moment ago to be written down on the phone (an archive, a message with big files), then sends everything at once (the queue first, then the outbox); the app calls it on `pagehide`, and on `visibilitychange` to hidden on touch screens only. Both single-flight runs (`queue.flush`, `flushOutbox`) let go of their place in the same breath as their last look at the list, so a run asked for while they finish is never answered by one that has already looked. `core/resilient.ts` wraps the IndexedDB store: a lost connection is opened again and the job repeated once; storage that will not come back is replaced by memory for the visit (`state.storage`, the Inbox banner). `core/diag.ts` is the list of what went wrong (addresses taken out of every text), `core/health.ts` turns state into the Health page's findings and the problem report, `ui/Crash.tsx` is the error boundary and `ui/recover.ts` the plain-DOM "could not start" screen. The service worker (`public/post/sw.js`) opens the saved copy of the app after three seconds of silence, or at once when the server answers with a 5xx.
- Seasons: `core/seasons.ts` (pure: `seasonOn(date)`, `activeSeason(mode, date)`, `piecesFor(season, n)`, one `SeasonLook` per season) and `ui/Seasons.tsx` (the glyph drawings, `SeasonLayer`, `SeasonBadge`). `Themed` in `ui/App.tsx` renders the layer over the app (`.sz`, z-index 4: under the floating bars at 5 and sheets at 50, `pointer-events: none`) and provides the season to `Avatar`, which shows the badge for people only. Settings are `settings.seasonal` `{ mode, motion, colours, badge }`. Animation is transform and opacity only, and `.pc` is `display: none` outside `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`.
- Looks: `Look` (`'refined' | 'calm'`, `core/settings.ts`) is saved with the other settings and set on the app root as `data-look`. The base of `post.css` is Refined and Calm is a block of overrides at the end of the file (`.post[data-look="calm"] …`), so every screen has both and a new screen needs no second stylesheet. Colours are CSS variables from `themeVars(settings, dark)` (`--bg --card --ink --mu --ln --ac --on --at --tint --ti --ok --bad` and the four avatar ones); shapes, shadows and fonts are fixed variables at the top of the file (`--r-card`, `--r-ctl`, `--pill`, `--font`). `lookChange()` brings a look's own accent along when it is switched, unless a colour was picked by hand. A sender's avatar colour is `senderHue(address, name)` in `core/format.ts` (a hash of the address over ten hues) with a tone from `avatarTone(look, dark)`; the unit tests check that every ground, text and accent, and the letters on every avatar hue, pass 4.5:1 in both looks, light, dark and pure black. A small control keeps a 44 px area for a thumb with an invisible ring (`::after { inset: -Npx }`) that never covers a neighbour. What floats over a list (`.tabs`, `.fab`, `.bar`) has `z-index: 5`, above the sticky day headings (`.sec`, 3), so a heading scrolling under a bar neither paints over it nor takes the tap meant for it.
- Folders: `core/folders.ts` (pure, tested in `folders.test.ts`) gives Outlook's folders Post's own names by Graph's well-known names (never by the language of the mailbox: `FOLDER_NAME`, `GRAPH_NAME`), builds the list with your own folders nested (`buildFolders`, from `graph.folderTree()`), turns a Graph message into a `Mail` that knows its folder (`fk` the kind, `fid` the id) and who it went to (`folderMail`, `recipientsOf`), and says which buttons a message gets in which folder (`placeActions`, `moveTargets`). The open folder is `state.folder` in the controller (`openFolder`, `moreInFolder` by `@odata.nextLink`, `refreshFolder`, a 20 s freshness rule, an answer for a folder that is no longer open is dropped), the list of folders is `loadFolders()` (45 s freshness, kept in the store as `<account>|foldertree`). Mail seen in a folder is kept in memory only (like search results) and is acted on by `relocate()`: archive, delete and `moveTo()` are the queue's `archive`/`delete`/`move` ops (`move` carries the folder id) with the same Undo, and read/flag on it go through `editCopy()` plus a queued op. Drafts are edited at Outlook in place (`graph.updateDraft()` PATCHes only the parts named in `edited`, a list of `DraftField`s, and nothing at all when none was changed; `graph.addToDraft()` puts new files on a draft) and sent with the existing exactly-once `deliver()` (kind `'draft'`: only files that are new are added), and `draftsOnTheirWay` lists a draft again at once when Undo is used or Outlook refuses the send; a draft that Outlook no longer has (404 on send) is handed back as a message of its own in the main slot. Where unsent text lives: `post.draft` (and `draft-files`) is only the message being written here (new, reply, forward); the unsent changes to an Outlook draft are one key, `post.draftedits`, a map from `<account>|<draftId>` to the changes (files in the store as `<account>|draftfiles|<id>`), swept after 30 days (`EDITS_KEPT_MS`, `sweepEdits`) and with the account. `Draft.modified` is Outlook's `lastModifiedDateTime` when the text was read: if it has moved on, Compose asks which one to go on with. The folder lookups of `graph.folderTree()` go to Outlook four at a time (`BATCH_WIDTH`, Outlook answers at most four concurrent calls per mailbox with 429), a slowed-down answer is retried and honours Retry-After, and a plain archive from the inbox does not read the folder list again (a move into a folder does). Deleting for good (`controller.eraseMail`, `controller.emptyFolder`, `graph.idsIn`, `graph.erase`): the only change with no queue and no Undo. It lists every message id of the folder from Outlook (`$select=id`, 200 a page, at most 5000 per go), leaves out mail that is waiting for a move (`leavingKeys`), and calls `POST /me/messages/{id}/permanentDelete` four at a time (`batchRetrying`, ten calls per chunk of 40 so progress shows in `state.erasing`); a 404/410 counts as gone, a 400/405/501 falls back to the ordinary `DELETE`, and a chunk that deletes nothing ends the run (`Deleted n of m`). Afterwards the folder and the folder counts are read again. `Erasing` is shown by `StatusBanners`; the question is `EraseSheet` in `ui/Folders.tsx`. Screens: `ui/Folders.tsx` (the Folders tab, `FolderList` with its Select mode and Empty bar, the desktop `SidebarFolders`), `ui/Move.tsx` (`MoveSheet`, `moveWay`), the Reader's far-mail mode (`far = !here`) and Compose's `'draft'` mode.
- Blocking and clean-up: `core/tidy.ts` (pure, tested in `tidy.test.ts` and `controller.folders.test.ts`) holds the matching (`isBlocked`, `blockedMail`, the same `ruleFor` rules as Sorting, so a company key `@domain` covers sub-domains but a shared provider only ever blocks one address) and the "kept" marks (`keptKey` = account|sender|received, the last 400): what you moved back to the inbox, or undid a tidy-up of, is never tidied again. State: `blocked` (saved in the `blocked` meta key; the setting `autoClean` in `post.settings`). `blockSender` also writes the override `promo` for the key, so the existing `pushTaught` sync keeps blocked mail out of the icon number and the alert server needs no change. `autoTidy` runs after every sync's `sortInBackground` (and when the setting is turned on): blocked mail first, then `staleThreads` (the Clean up rows older than the chosen days, with sorted marks, not kept, not flagged); one at a time (`tidying`), never over a toast that has an Undo, each as one `relocate` with an Undo that writes the kept marks. Graph `messageRules` would give a server-side block but needs MailboxSettings.ReadWrite.
- Tabs: `tabSwipe` in `apps/web/src/lib/swipe.ts` (pure, tested) decides whether a touch on the tab row was a hard quick flick to the left; `CategoryTabs` in `ui/Inbox.tsx` watches touch start and end only, so the row keeps its own scrolling (never put `touch-action` or pointer capture on that row).
- Sender rows: `core/digest.ts` (pure, tested) turns the list of conversations into one row per sender (a `Thread` with `members`); the Inbox screen uses it only for the three quiet tabs. `core/sweep.ts` decides which mail each Sweep choice reaches. Motion lives in the "polish 2" block of `post.css`, all of it under `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`.
- Subscriptions: `core/subscriptions.ts` (pure, tested) groups inbox mail by mailbox and sender and keeps the ones with bulk marks (`unsub`, `list`, `bulk`, or sorted as a promotion). The page is `ui/Subscriptions.tsx` (`#/settings/subscriptions`); it reuses the Sweep and Block sheets that now live in `ui/SenderSheets.tsx`. The unsubscribe link is read from the headers of the newest mail that has one, fetched when the sheet opens so the button can open the page without being blocked as a pop-up.
- Mute: `muted` (`{ key: threadKey, subject }`, kept in the store as meta `muted`, at most 200) and `mutedMail()` in `core/tidy.ts`; `muteConversation`/`unmute` in the controller, and `autoTidy` archives muted mail beside the blocked-sender tidy (same `kept` marks, same Undo). It is Post-side only, so the alert server does not know about it (an optional later step would sync the muted keys to it).
- Where did my mail go: `core/leftlog.ts` (`diffInbox`, `LeftEntry`, kept as meta `leftlog`, at most 80). `reload()` compares the list it showed with the new one; `relocate(..., why)` and `applyOverrides` say what took mail away, and a disappearance with no reason is Outlook's (`quietNext` skips one comparison when a mailbox is removed or a block's own tab move happens). `sync()` reads the hidden marks of up to 20 new messages before the first `reload()` (`presortMs`, 2.5 s at most). `traceMoved()` asks `graph.whereIs()` (a `$filter` on receivedDateTime and sender over `/me/messages`) for up to five removed messages and stores `where` on the entry; `tellMoved()` is the toast for a tab change. The can is the `onTrash` prop of `SwipeRow` (`.rowtrash`).
- New folders and dragging: `graph.createFolder` (POST, sent once), `controller.createFolder` / `moveToNewFolder`, `ui/NewFolder.tsx` (the sheet), `core/drop.ts` (pure, tested: where a message may be dropped), `ui/Carry.tsx` (`useCarry` is the phone strip, `DropButton` the sidebar target for the mouse). `SwipeRow` lifts a row after `LIFT_MS` of holding still (finger events, since React cannot cancel the page scroll) and reports where the finger is and where it let go; on a mouse the row is `draggable`.
- `apps/web/src/post/ui/` are the screens (`Attachments.tsx` the file list and viewer, `pdf.ts` the lazily loaded PDF drawing); `idb.ts` is the IndexedDB store; `push.ts` the push and icon number code; `public/post/sw.js` the service worker.
- `apps/web/worker/upload-relay.js` is the one place where this site's own server touches mail: it passes a slice of a big attachment to Outlook's upload address when the browser may not (tested in `upload-relay.test.mjs`, run by `npm test`).
- `supabase/functions/post-alerts/` is the server (sign-in, sessions, change notifications, alert rules, token minting); `npm test` in `supabase/`.
- Alerts follow the Primary tab by running **the app's own sorting on the server**: `supabase/functions/post-alerts/classify.ts` is a generated copy of `apps/web/src/post/core/classify.ts` (written by `supabase/build-functions.mjs`, which only swaps the one import of the app's `Kind` type for the type itself), and the paste file `supabase/dashboard/post-alerts.ts` carries it inline. After touching `classify.ts` (or `types.ts`'s `Kind`), run `npm run build:functions` in `supabase/`; the tests in `supabase/` and `apps/web` fail while a copy is stale, so the app and the server cannot drift apart. What only the phone knows reaches the server through one more call, `taught` (`core/server.ts` `taught()`): `{ set?, remove?, extra? }`, where `set` maps `anna@x.no` or `@x.no` to a kind (the senders and companies moved to another tab, from `controller.applyOverrides`), `remove` lists keys to forget and `extra` is `none | codes | transactions` (what counts besides Primary, default `codes`). The function holds ONE list per mailbox for all of the person's devices, as a `post_config` row `taught:<account id>` (service role only, no SQL; removed with the account), and `alertTaughtPatch()` merges what a device sends (forget first, then add). A device never sends "the whole list": it sends what changed since it last got something through, which it remembers in the meta key `taughtSent` (per mailbox), so a computer that has moved nothing cannot wipe what the phone moved (`controller.pushTaught()`, one mailbox and one run at a time, silent when it fails, so a call that never got through is repeated at the next look). When `status` says the server holds no rules at all (`rules_digest` of the empty list) although this device sent some, it sends them all again; an answer of 400 is not repeated until the choices change; a mailbox that needs signing in is skipped; and a start that could not read the saved choices sends nothing. `status` answers with `smart: 1` (the code level; a server without it is the older code, so the phone sends nothing and Settings says **Needs update**) and, per mailbox, `extra` and `rules_digest`, a fingerprint (`rulesDigest()`, the same function on both sides) of the union the server holds. New mail is decided in `alertVerdict()`: `alertSort()` (the app's `classify()` with the headers, the first 200 characters of `bodyPreview`, the moved senders as overrides and the VIP list), `alertCounts()` (Primary counts; a security code or sign-in alert counts with `codes` or `transactions`; all of Transactions counts with `transactions`; when the sorting throws, the mail counts), then `alertDecide()` for mode, read state, folder, VIPs and hours. A Sent Items lookup (`alertKnownSender()`: `toRecipients/any`, a `$search` only when Microsoft refuses that filter with a 400, five seconds per call) is made only when "you have written to this sender" would change the kind, never for an `isRobotAddress()` sender, and once per mailbox and sender in a batch of notifications; if it fails or is too slow, the mail counts. Codes are told in `classify.ts` in three tiers: `SECURITY_SURE` and `SECURITY_CODE` (code words, and digits next to "your ... code is"; also believed in bulk mail, unless it sells something) and `SECURITY_WEAK` ("log in", "password", "new device": only for robot or role mail that is not an offer, a receipt or a parcel, never in bulk mail). The server rules are tested in `supabase/tests/post-alerts.test.ts` and `post-alerts-handler.test.ts`, the phone's side in `core/controller.test.ts`, `core/server.test.ts` and `core/classify.test.ts`.
